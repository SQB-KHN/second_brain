# Campaign Plan

Plans a campaign with the funnel maths done first and the kill criteria agreed before launch, which are the two things most plans leave out.

---

## Who this is for

Anyone who has to spend a marketing budget and answer for it: growth and performance marketers, marketing managers, founders running their own campaigns, and agency account leads planning for clients.

## The problem it solves

Most campaign plans are a list of channels and a budget split. Two things that decide whether the campaign works are missing.

**Whether the numbers can possibly reach the target.** Plenty of campaigns launch with a budget that cannot produce the outcome, and nobody notices until it is spent.

**What result means stop.** After launch, nobody wants to kill their own campaign. Every underperforming campaign gets one more week, repeatedly, until the money is gone.

This settles both before launch. It takes an hour and regularly saves an entire budget.

---

## What you give it

**It will insist on the first one:**

| Input | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Objective as a number with a date** | Awareness is not an objective. 200 qualified leads by 30 November is. A campaign without a number cannot be judged and therefore runs forever. |
| Budget | And whether it can scale if something works |
| Timeline | Including any fixed date you are tied to |
| Audience | Cold, warm or existing customers. These need completely different plans. |
| What already exists | Landing page, creative, list, and previous campaign results |

**Previous results are the most valuable input you can give it.** They replace assumed conversion rates with your own numbers, which changes the funnel maths from a guess into a forecast.

**One objective per campaign.** If you name several, it will say that multiple objectives means no objective and ask which one you would keep.

## What you get back

1. The objective, as a number and a date
2. **The funnel maths**, each stage, each rate, and whether it is your history or an assumption
3. A plain verdict on whether your budget can reach your target
4. Channel plan with the reason for each channel
5. Message by funnel stage: cold, warm, hot
6. Creative requirements, including minimum volume before a variant can be judged
7. Measurement: primary metric, guardrails, cadence, first review date
8. **Kill and scale criteria**, as specific numbers and dates
9. Budget phasing: test, trigger, scale, reserve
10. Every assumption listed, with what happens if it is wrong

---

## The funnel maths comes first

It works backwards from your objective through each conversion stage to the budget required, then compares that against the budget you have, and tells you which of three things is true:

- The maths works with room to spare
- The maths works only if every rate holds, which is fragile
- The maths does not work, and the target, budget or timeline has to change

The third one gets said first and clearly. A plan that quietly assumes your conversion rate will double is not a plan, and this is the step that catches it before anything is spent.

## Kill criteria, set before launch

Written down in advance: what number, by what date, means stop. And what number means scale.

Specific enough that two people would read them the same way. "If CPA is above 900 rupees after 500 clicks, pause and rework the landing page" is a kill criterion. "If it is not working we will review" is not.

This is the section every plan skips and the one that saves the most money.

## Message by stage

The most common campaign failure is running the same message everywhere.

| Stage | What it needs |
|---|---|
| **Cold** | The problem. They do not know they need you, so leading with your differentiation lands on nobody. |
| **Warm** | The differentiation. They know the problem and are choosing between options. |
| **Hot** | The offer and the risk reversal. They are convinced and looking for a reason not to hesitate. |

## What good output looks like

A strong plan makes you uncomfortable in the funnel maths section and relieved by the end of it. Either it shows your budget cannot reach your target, which is worth knowing now, or it shows the plan holds only if a specific rate stays above a specific number, which tells you exactly what to watch.

The kill criteria should be numbers you would genuinely act on. If reading them makes you want to soften one, that is the one to keep.

## Using it

Give it the objective, the budget and the timeline.

Examples that work:

- "Plan a campaign. 3 lakh budget, six weeks, need 150 demo bookings. Here are last quarter's numbers."
- "We are launching in September. Cold audience, no list, 5 lakh to spend. What is realistic?"
- "Here is our plan. Check whether the budget can actually deliver the target."
- "Our last campaign ran four months past the point it should have been killed. Plan this one properly."

## Limits worth knowing

- It does not promise a result. It shows what would have to be true, and says clearly when the maths does not work.
- It does not write creative or copy.
- It does not buy media or set up campaigns.
- It will not invent benchmark conversion rates and present them as fact. Assumed rates are labelled as assumptions every single time, so you can see exactly what the plan is resting on.
- It only knows the channels your team can execute if you tell it. A plan requiring video you cannot produce is not a plan.

## If something looks wrong

| What you see | Usual cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| It says the budget cannot reach the target | The funnel maths does not close | That is the finding. Change the target, the budget or the timeline. |
| Too many assumptions flagged | No historical data supplied | Give it previous campaign results. It swaps assumptions for your own rates. |
| It refuses to start | Objective is not a number | Give it a number and a date, or ask it to help derive one from a business target. |
| Channel split feels conservative | It only includes what your team can execute | Tell it what capacity you actually have. |
| Kill criteria feel harsh | Working as intended | These are easier to agree now than to enforce in week six. |
