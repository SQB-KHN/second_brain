# Funnel and Landing Page Audit

Improving a page is the cheapest performance gain available to a marketer and the one most often done by guessing. Somebody dislikes the headline, the headline changes, nothing happens. This skill finds where people actually leave, works out why, and ranks the fixes so effort goes where the loss is.

## When to use this

Trigger on: landing page review, conversion rate problem, funnel audit, "people are not converting", "should I redesign this page", checkout drop-off, form abandonment.

## Before you start

Required: the page content. Pasted copy, a description of the layout, or a screenshot.

Ask for, and use if available:
1. **Funnel numbers.** Visitors, page views, form starts, form completions, purchases. Without these the audit is informed opinion. With them it is diagnosis.
2. Where the traffic comes from, and what the ad or email promised. Message match is the single largest cause of bounce and cannot be assessed without knowing the promise.
3. Mobile share of traffic, which in most Indian markets is the majority and changes which problems matter.
4. What the page has to achieve, one specific action.

If numbers are unavailable, say plainly that the audit is based on the page alone, and that the ranked fixes are informed judgement rather than evidence of where users leave.

## Workflow

### 1. Find the leak before reading the page

Where numbers exist, compute the drop at each step and find the largest one first.

Fix the biggest leak, not the most visible problem. A headline everyone dislikes on a page that converts at 8 percent matters less than a form that loses two-thirds of the people who start it.

State the leak in both percentage and absolute terms. Losing 40 percent at a step that only 50 people reach is worth less than losing 12 percent at a step that 5,000 reach.

### 2. Check message match first

Compare what the ad, email or search result promised against what the page says in its first screen.

If the promise and the page do not match, stop here. Nothing else on the page matters, because the visitor concluded they were in the wrong place within a few seconds. This is the most common and most fixable cause of a bad conversion rate, and it is invisible if you only look at the page.

### 3. Audit the first screen

A visitor decides in a few seconds. In that time the page must answer three things:

- What is this
- Who is it for
- What do I do next

Judge whether all three are answerable without scrolling. If any is missing, that is a finding, and it usually outranks everything below it.

### 4. Work through the page

**Clarity of the single action.** One primary action. Multiple competing calls to action mean no call to action, and secondary links compete with the thing you actually want.

**Proof against claims.** For each claim on the page, ask what supports it. Claims without proof read as noise. Note where a testimonial, number or demonstration is missing rather than suggesting one be invented.

**Objection handling.** Take the top objections, ideally from customer research, and check whether the page addresses them. Unhandled objections do not stop people, they just make them leave to think about it, which is the same outcome.

**Friction.** Count and question every form field, since each one costs completions. Flag required account creation before value is delivered, unclear or hidden pricing, unexpected costs appearing late, and anything that asks for effort before giving a reason to make it.

**Mobile.** Where mobile is the majority of traffic, check for the specific failures: long forms, tables that do not fit, small tap targets, key content pushed below several screens of scroll, and pop-ups that cover the page.

**Trust.** Contact details, a real address, policies, security signals at the point of payment, and recognisable names where they genuinely exist.

**Speed and technical.** These cannot be measured from the content, so flag them as checks the user should run rather than making a claim about them.

### 5. Rank the fixes

Place each fix on impact against effort, and group them:

- **Do now.** High impact, low effort. Copy, headline, form fields, message match. Usually where most of the available gain sits.
- **Plan.** High impact, high effort. Page restructure, new proof assets, pricing changes.
- **Ignore for now.** Low impact regardless of effort. This section matters, because it stops a team spending a week on something that will not move anything.

### 6. Never promise a lift

Do not write that a fix will increase conversion by a particular percentage. Nobody can know that, and a number invented here will be quoted back later as though it were a forecast.

Say what the fix addresses and what evidence supports the concern. Then recommend testing it, and say which metric would show whether it worked. For anything where the traffic is high enough to test properly, say so.

## Output format

**1. Verdict.** Two lines. Where the loss is and what the first fix should be.

**2. Funnel table.** Each step, volume, drop percentage, and absolute loss. Only where numbers exist.

**3. Message match.** What was promised against what the page says.

**4. First screen assessment.** The three questions, answered or not.

**5. Findings.** Each one with what is wrong, why it matters, and the evidence or reasoning behind it.

**6. Ranked fixes.** Do now, plan, ignore for now.

**7. What to test and how you will know.** The metric that would move, and whether traffic is sufficient to test properly.

**8. What could not be assessed.** Speed, technical behaviour, actual mobile rendering, and anything else requiring access.

## Quality bar

- If numbers were supplied, is the biggest leak identified before any page opinion is offered
- Is message match checked before anything else on the page
- Does every finding carry a reason rather than a preference
- Are fixes ranked by impact against effort, with a genuine ignore-for-now list
- Is there no promised lift percentage anywhere in the output
- Is everything that could not be assessed stated plainly rather than skipped

## What this skill does not do

- It does not visit your page. It works on what you paste, describe or screenshot.
- It does not measure page speed, test rendering, or check technical behaviour. Those are flagged for you to check.
- It does not promise a conversion lift from any change.
- It does not redesign the page or write the new copy, though it will say specifically what each element needs to do.
- Without funnel numbers it is informed judgement, and it will say so rather than implying evidence it does not have.
