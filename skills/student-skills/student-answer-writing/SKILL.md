# Answer Writing and Review

Two students know the same amount and get different marks. The difference is not knowledge. One of them wrote an answer an examiner could mark quickly, and the other wrote everything they knew in a single paragraph. Nobody teaches this, and it is worth more marks than most of the content students spend their time on.

## When to use this

Trigger on: answer writing, how to write this answer, model answer, marking scheme, presentation marks, word limit, structuring an answer, subjective paper, "why did I lose marks", "how should I attempt this question".

## Before you start

Required: the question, and either your attempt at it or a request for the structure.

Ask, briefly:
1. **Marks available, and expected length.** A three-mark and a fifteen-mark answer on the same topic are entirely different documents, and writing the long one for three marks costs the time you needed elsewhere.
2. The exam and level, since marking conventions differ between boards, universities and competitive exams.
3. **The command word, exactly as printed.** Explain, describe, discuss, derive, compare, evaluate and justify all earn marks differently.
4. Whether a marking scheme or model answer exists. If so, mark against theirs rather than a general standard.
5. Time available per question, since a technically better answer you cannot finish is worth less.

## What this will and will not do

It helps you write **your own** answers better: structure, what earns marks, what wastes time, and where your attempt lost marks.

**It will not write an answer for you to submit.** Not an assignment, a project, an internal, or a take-home paper. The reason is not moralising. An answer you did not write teaches you nothing, and the marks you get for it are the last ones you will get on that topic.

## Workflow

### 1. Read the command word first, because it decides the structure

| Command | What earns the marks |
|---|---|
| State, list, name | The item. Nothing else earns anything |
| Define | Precision, usually a specific form of words |
| Describe | What it is, in order, with its features |
| Explain | The mechanism and the because. A description scores badly here |
| Derive, prove | Every step shown. A correct final answer with steps missing loses most of the marks |
| Compare | Both sides against the same stated criteria. Two separate descriptions score badly |
| Discuss, evaluate | Both directions and a position. One-sided answers cap out |
| Justify | A claim plus the reason. The reason carries the marks |

An answer that describes when asked to explain contains correct material and scores poorly, and the student concludes they did not know the topic well enough. They knew it. They answered a different question.

### 2. Match the length to the marks, and show the arithmetic

Roughly one creditable point per mark, unless the scheme says otherwise. A five-mark question wants five things, not a page of everything you know.

State what the length should be, and say plainly that **writing beyond what the marks justify costs the time other questions needed.** Over-writing is one of the commonest and least recognised causes of a low total.

### 3. Structure so the marks are findable

An examiner marking two hundred scripts either finds a mark or does not:

- One point per line or short paragraph, never five points inside one paragraph
- The direct answer first, then elaboration, for anything not requiring a derivation
- Key term underlined or headed, where the exam allows it
- A diagram where a diagram is faster than words, labelled, and referred to from the text
- Steps numbered where sequence matters

**The best answer is not the most impressive one. It is the one whose marks a tired examiner cannot miss.**

### 4. Say what to cut

An introduction restating the question. Definitions of terms the question already used. Everything you know that was not asked. Repetition in the conclusion.

This is where most wasted time in a paper goes, and students protect it because it feels like effort.

### 5. In review mode, separate three different losses

For a student's attempt: what earned marks, what did not and why, and what the next mark specifically required. Then split the loss, because each needs a different fix:

- **Content missing.** You did not know it. That is a study problem.
- **Content present, not credited.** You knew it and wrote it in a way that did not score: buried in a paragraph, unlabelled, or answering a different command word.
- **Time.** You ran out, or spent it elsewhere.

**The second is the one worth finding.** It is the fastest marks a student can gain and it is invisible without somebody pointing at it.

### 6. Handle diagrams, formulae and working properly

Formula stated before substitution, since it usually carries a mark of its own. Units at every stage and in the final answer. Working shown even when the answer is obvious, because method marks survive an arithmetic slip and a bare answer does not. Diagrams labelled and referred to rather than left to speak for themselves.

### 7. Practise under the real constraint

An answer written with unlimited time is not evidence about an exam. Where a student is practising, state the time this answer should take and suggest attempting it under that constraint before any review.

## Output format

**Structure mode:** what the command word requires; length and mark arithmetic; the structure in order, with what earns marks in each part; what to include and what to cut; diagram, formula and working requirements; the time this should take.

**Review mode:**

**1. Marks as they would be awarded**, against the scheme where supplied.

**2. Where each mark came from**, quoted from your answer.

**3. What the next mark needed**, specifically.

**4. Content missing, content not credited, and time.** Separated.

**5. The two changes** that would gain the most marks next time.

**6. What you did well**, and should keep doing.

## Quality bar

- Is the command word identified and the structure built from it
- Is length tied to the marks, with the arithmetic stated
- Is the answer structured so individual marks are findable rather than buried in prose
- Is there an explicit list of what to cut
- In review, are content missing, content not credited and time loss separated
- Is "what the next mark needed" stated specifically rather than generally
- Are formula, units and working requirements stated where the subject needs them
- Is the time this answer should take stated
- **Is nothing produced that could be submitted as the student's own work**
- Could the student say in one sentence what to do differently in the next answer

## What this skill does not do

- **It does not write answers for you to submit.** Not assignments, projects, internals or take-home papers. It shows structure and reviews your attempt.
- It does not know your board's or university's marking conventions unless you supply the scheme. Where you do, it applies yours.
- It does not guarantee marks. Examiners vary; it says what a scheme rewards.
- It does not teach the topic. Where the loss is content missing, that is a study problem and it says so.
- It cannot see handwriting, presentation, or a diagram you describe in words rather than draw.
