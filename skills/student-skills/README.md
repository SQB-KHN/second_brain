# Students Skills for Claude

Five skills for studying rather than for producing work: where the marks actually are, whether you really know it, how to write it, what to do with your time, and why you keep getting the same things wrong.

They run inside your own Claude account, on past papers and your own attempts. Nothing to install on your machine, nothing to connect, no coding.

---

## Who these are for

Anyone studying for something that gets marked: class 9 to 12 students across boards, competitive exam aspirants for engineering, medical, civil services, management and banking, undergraduates and postgraduates, working professionals studying part-time, and anyone self-studying without a coaching institute doing the analysis for them.

Four of the five need no file at all. They work on what you type, so a student with a phone and a syllabus can use them.

## The five skills

| Skill | Give it | Get back |
|---|---|---|
| **[Past Paper and Weightage Analysis](student-exam-analysis/student-exam-analysis-guide.md)** | Past papers | Weightage in marks, reliability bands, repeated patterns, and what to finish first |
| **[Understanding Check](student-understanding-check/student-understanding-check-guide.md)** | Your own explanation of a topic | Exactly where it breaks, and whether you understood, memorised or only recognised it |
| **[Answer Writing and Review](student-answer-writing/student-answer-writing-guide.md)** | A question and your attempt | Structure built from the command word, and what you knew but failed to get credited for |
| **[Study Plan and Revision Schedule](student-study-plan/student-study-plan-guide.md)** | The syllabus and your real hours | Honest arithmetic first, three spaced passes, and room for the week that goes wrong |
| **[Mistake Analysis](student-mistake-log/student-mistake-log-guide.md)** | Your wrong answers and your reasoning | Concept, application, execution or time, the pattern across tests, and three ranked fixes |

Each skill has its own guide with the exact inputs it needs, the phrasings that trigger it, what good output looks like, its limits, and a troubleshooting table.

## The line none of these cross

**Nothing you could submit.**

No essay, no assignment, no project, no lab record, no exam answer written for you. These five analyse, plan, test and review **your own** work, and none of them produces something you could hand in.

That is not moralising, and it is not about getting caught. An answer you did not write teaches you nothing, and the marks you get for it are the last ones you will get on that topic. The gap it papers over turns up in the exam hall, where nothing can help you.

There is a second rule that runs through all five, and it is the one that makes them different from just asking. **Find the gap, do not fill it.** Ask any AI about a concept and it will explain it well, which feels enormously helpful and is close to useless, because a good explanation you read becomes one more thing you will recognise later and be unable to produce. So the understanding check makes you explain first and withholds its own explanation until you ask for it.

## Why you will not find "summarise my notes" here

Because asking Claude to summarise notes, explain a concept, solve a problem or make flashcards already works, unaided, and works well. A skill there would add ceremony rather than capability.

What does not already work is the part around the studying. Knowing which chapters the exam has actually rewarded for ten years. Knowing whether you understand something or have only recognised it. Knowing whether the marks you lost were knowledge you lacked or knowledge you failed to present. Knowing how many hours you genuinely have before you build a plan on hours you do not. And knowing whether the same mistake has now appeared in four tests.

None of that is a writing task, and all of it is what actually decides a result.

## Why these five

**Weightage analysis.** The syllabus implies twelve chapters matter equally. The exam has disagreed for ten years, in papers nobody analysed. **Understanding check.** Reading it and it making sense is the most reliable liar in studying, and every ordinary check makes it worse.

**Answer writing.** Two students know the same amount and score differently, because one wrote an answer an examiner could mark quickly. **Study plan.** Timetables fail for three structural reasons, none of them discipline: too few hours, one pass instead of several, and no room for a bad week.

**Mistake analysis.** The same errors recur because reviewing a test means feeling bad and moving on, and "silly mistake" is not a category.

## They work together

Each stands alone. In sequence they are a full study cycle:

```
Past papers        →  Weightage Analysis    →  where the marks actually are
That, plus time    →  Study Plan            →  hours allocated by return, spaced
While studying     →  Understanding Check   →  whether it went in, or only felt like it
Practice attempts  →  Answer Writing        →  what you knew and did not get credited for
After each test    →  Mistake Analysis      →  the pattern, and three fixes
```

Three pairings worth knowing. Run the weightage analysis before the study plan, because without it the plan allocates by chapter length, which is the thing it is trying to avoid. Feed the mistake analysis back into the plan, since its concept errors are next month's topics. And when the answer review says your loss was content not credited rather than content missing, that is an answer-writing fix and not a reason to restudy.

## What makes these different from asking directly

**Marks, never counts, and permission to leave things.** Ten one-mark questions and two five-mark questions carry the same weight and need different preparation, so a count-based weightage table is wrong in a way that costs a month. And the "never appeared" band names what is in your syllabus and absent from ten years of papers. Deciding not to study something is a decision students are never given permission to make, and it is where their time comes from; it arrives with two honest warnings rather than as a licence.

**It withholds the explanation.** You explain, it finds the break, and it explains only when you ask. That order is the entire design.

**Four states, not a grade, and the gap is often below the topic.** Understood, memorised, recognised or missing: all four feel similar while you study, and each wastes a different amount of time if misdiagnosed. And where your explanation breaks in a way that points downward, it names the prerequisite, because studying the current topic harder will not fix a gap sitting underneath it.

**The command word decides the structure.** An answer that describes when asked to explain is correct material scoring badly, and the student concludes they did not know it well enough. They knew it; they answered a different question. **Content missing against content not credited** is the same distinction from the other side, and the second is the fastest marks any student can gain, requiring no new knowledge and invisible without somebody pointing at it.

**The hours arithmetic before the schedule.** Where the syllabus does not fit the time, that gets said before a plan is written rather than discovered in month two.

**If you did not close the book, it was not revision.** Every planned session ends with something retrieved from memory, because re-reading produces exactly the recognition feeling that walks students into exams confident and blank.

**"Silly mistake" is not a category, and one instance is noise.** Calling execution errors silly implies they are unfixable, which is precisely why they never get fixed. And a pattern is only claimed where something appears more than once, so you do not restudy a topic you already knew.

## Getting started

1. Take one skill's folder and compress it to a `.zip`. The file inside must stay named `SKILL.md`, because that is what Claude looks for. The guide beside it is not needed and can be left out.
2. Go to **Customize**, then **Skills**, then add a skill and upload the zip.
3. Repeat for each skill you want.

Only the weightage analysis and the mistake analysis use code execution. The other three need nothing, so most of this set runs on a free plan. Skills you upload stay private to your account, and once installed they work in Chat and Cowork.

**Where to start.** Take a topic you are confident about and run the understanding check on it. Writing your own explanation and seeing where it breaks takes ten minutes and is usually the moment this set makes sense.

## Using them

Paste the papers, the question, or your own explanation.

- "Six years of past papers, class 12 physics. Where are the marks?"
- "Here is how I would explain electromagnetic induction. Where does it break?"
- "Five-mark 'explain' question, here is my answer. Where did I lose marks?"
- "Class 12 boards in 90 days, school until 3, coaching 5 to 8. Six subjects."
- "18 wrong in this mock. Here is what I was thinking on each one."

## What these will not do

- **Nothing you could submit.** No essay, assignment, project, lab record or exam answer written for you. Not for any reason, in any version.
- **No explaining before you have tried.** The understanding check asks for your explanation first and withholds its own until you ask.
- **No prediction.** It will not forecast your score, your rank, or what will be asked this year. You get reliability and patterns, which are what you can plan against.
- **No telling you to skip things, and no false reassurance.** It shows the return on each topic and what leaving it would cost, with the decision yours. And it will not say you understand something when your explanation shows you do not.
- **No solving your problems or teaching your topics.** Where the gap is knowledge, it names the gap and sends you to study it.
- **No system access, and nothing sent.** These work on what you paste, and every output is for you.

## What you need

- A Claude account, with code execution turned on for two of the five
- Past papers and your own attempts, pasted or typed
- Nothing else. No installation, no terminal, no coding, nothing to connect
