# Forecast Commit Brief

A forecast gets submitted as a single number with no working behind it, and then defended monthly in a meeting where the only available moves are to hold the number or lose credibility. The document that prevents that argument is one page and takes twenty minutes, and almost nobody writes it.

## When to use this

Trigger on: forecast, commit, submit the number, forecast call, sales commit, pipeline commit, monthly forecast, quarter close, QBR, "what number am I giving", "will we hit it".

## Before you start

Required: the deal list with your own assessment of each, or the output of a pipeline review.

Ask, briefly:
1. Who receives this, and what they do with it. A number that feeds a hiring plan carries a different obligation from one that feeds a dashboard.
2. The target, and the period.
3. Whether they want a single number or a range. If a single number, this brief still shows the range and names which one is being committed.
4. **What was committed last period, and what actually landed.** This is the most useful input available, because a forecaster's credibility is their track record.
5. Anything already known that has not reached the pipeline: a verbal yes, a budget freeze, a reorganisation, a competitor win.

## The one rule that matters

**Commit what you can defend, then say what would have to be true for the rest.**

A forecast is not a prediction. It is a commitment with evidence behind it. The failure in both directions is the same failure: a number stated without its working. Sandbagging and happy ears are both undefended numbers, and both destroy the forecast's usefulness, because the receiver cannot tell which one they are being handed.

So every number in this brief carries the deals behind it and the condition attached to it.

## Workflow

### 1. Build three tiers from evidence, not from stage

- **Commit.** You would stake your credibility on it, and the evidence supports the date. Name the criteria and apply them consistently across every deal.
- **Best case.** Would close if a specific named thing happens. Name the thing per deal, never in general.
- **Upside.** Everything else, and stated plainly as not forecast.

Show the deals in each tier with their values. A tier without its deal list is an assertion, and an assertion is what the meeting will argue with.

### 2. State what you are deliberately not counting

This is the section that buys the most credibility and gets written the least.

List the deals that look good and are not in commit, with the reason for each: no economic buyer met, the paper process will not fit inside the period, slipped twice already, single-threaded.

**A forecaster who names what they are excluding gets trusted on what they include.** That is the whole mechanism, and it works in one direction only: you cannot claim the credibility without doing the exclusion.

### 3. Name the deals that carry it

State the concentration. "Three deals are 58 percent of commit." Then one line per deal: what is left to happen, who owns it, by when.

If the commit rests on one deal, say so in the first paragraph. A number that is really one deal should be presented as one deal with a probability, not laundered into a number.

### 4. Write what would have to be true to miss

Two or three specific conditions, not general risk. "If the security review on the largest deal takes more than three weeks it moves to next quarter, and commit drops by 40 lakh."

This is more useful than a confidence percentage, because it is actionable and because the receiver can decide whether to help with any of it. A percentage invites a negotiation about the percentage.

### 5. Say what you need, if anything

A reference call, an executive sponsor, a legal turnaround, a pricing approval. Each with the deal it unblocks and the value at stake.

A forecast brief that asks for nothing is either a very clean quarter or a rep who has stopped asking.

### 6. Account for your own track record

Where prior commits and actuals were supplied, state them: committed against landed, for the last two or three periods. If a pattern exists, name it.

A forecaster who consistently lands under commit should say so and adjust, and saying it is exactly what makes the next number believable. Do not hide the pattern, and do not over-correct it into a new form of sandbagging.

### 7. Keep it to one page

Commit, best case, upside, what is excluded, the deals that carry it, what would have to be true to miss, what you need. If it runs longer, the working belongs in the pipeline review rather than here.

## Output format

One page. Roughly 350 to 450 words.

```
# [Period] Forecast Commit

## The number
[Commit, best case, upside. Three figures, and which one is being committed.]

## What carries it
[The deals in commit, with concentration stated. One line each: what is left,
who owns it, by when.]

## What I am not counting
[Deals excluded from commit, with the reason for each.]

## What would have to be true to miss
[Two or three specific conditions, each with its value impact.]

## What I need
[Each ask with the deal it unblocks and the value at stake. Or "nothing".]

## Track record
[Committed against landed, last two or three periods. The pattern, if there is one.]
```

For a verbal forecast call, the same content in the order a manager will ask for it: the number, the concentration, the exclusions, the risks, the asks.

## Quality bar

- Does every tier show the deals behind it and their values
- Do commit, best case and upside sum to the open pipeline
- Is there a "what I am not counting" section with deals actually in it
- Does every best-case deal name the specific thing that has to happen
- Is concentration stated, and is it in the first paragraph where the commit rests on one or two deals
- Are the miss conditions specific and valued, rather than general risk
- Does the track record show committed against landed, where the data was supplied
- Is every ask attached to a deal and a value
- Does it fit on one page with every tier's deal list still on it
- Would the receiver be able to tell whether this number is sandbagged or optimistic

## What this skill does not do

- It does not produce the pipeline analysis. Give it the deal list and your assessment, or run the pipeline review first.
- It does not decide your commit for you. It shows what the evidence supports, and says plainly when your number sits above it.
- It does not inflate a number to please a receiver, or deflate one to stay safe. Both are undefended numbers.
- It does not advise on discount or terms to pull a deal into the period.
- It does not commit anything on your behalf. The number is yours, and it is your name on it.
