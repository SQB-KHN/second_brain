# Sales Skills for Claude

Five skills for the parts of selling that decide the outcome: whether the pipeline is real, whether the deal is real, what you did not learn on the call, where the account grows, and what number you can actually defend.

They run inside your own Claude account, on exports and notes you already have. Nothing to install on your machine, nothing to connect, no coding.

---

## Who these are for

People who carry a number: account executives and enterprise sellers, inside sales reps and SDRs, sales managers and team leads, regional and area sales managers, key account managers, channel and distributor managers, pre-sales and solution consultants, sales operations, counsellors and advisors in education, insurance and real estate, and founders doing their own selling.

Four of the five need no export at all. They work on notes and what you can tell them, so a rep with no CRM access and no admin rights can use them from the first day.

## The five skills

| Skill | Give it | Get back |
|---|---|---|
| **[Pipeline Review and Forecast Reality Check](sales-pipeline-review/sales-pipeline-review-guide.md)** | A CRM or pipeline export | Stage integrity, parked and slipped deals, coverage computed twice, evidence-based forecast tiers, and a ranked review agenda |
| **[Deal Qualification and Risk Review](sales-deal-qualification/sales-deal-qualification-guide.md)** | What has actually happened on one deal | Evidence separated from inference, every area rated, and the stage and close date the evidence supports |
| **[Discovery Plan and Call Debrief](sales-discovery-debrief/sales-discovery-debrief-guide.md)** | Who you are meeting, or your call notes | A sequenced question set with a real objective, or a debrief with the next call's agenda |
| **[Account Plan and Whitespace](sales-account-plan/sales-account-plan-guide.md)** | What the account buys, and who you know there | Computed concentration risk, a whitespace grid, relationship coverage, and plays ranked by winnability |
| **[Forecast Commit Brief](sales-forecast-commit/sales-forecast-commit-guide.md)** | Your deal list and your read on each | A one-page commit with the deals behind every tier, what you are not counting, and what would break it |

Each skill has its own guide with the exact inputs it needs, the phrasings that trigger it, what good output looks like, its limits, and a troubleshooting table.

## Why you will not find "write me a follow-up email" here

Because asking any AI for a follow-up email already works. A skill there would add ceremony, not capability.

What does not already work is the judgement around the selling. Whether a deal at 80 percent has anything behind it. Which four deals decide the quarter, before the review rather than in week eleven. What you failed to ask on a call, which stays invisible until it kills the deal. Which empty cell in an account is an opportunity and which just means nobody has asked. And what number you can put your name on. That is where sales is won and lost, and none of it is a writing problem.

## The line none of these cross

**No deal is more advanced than its evidence.**

A stage, a probability, a close date and a commit are each earned by something that happened. Where nothing happened, these skills say so rather than accepting the number in front of them.

That matters more here than it looks. Sales output feeds a forecast that a company hires, spends and plans against, so optimism dressed as assessment is not a personal habit, it is a business risk. And it runs both ways: sandbagging is the same failure as happy ears, because both are numbers stated without their working, and a receiver who cannot tell which they are getting discounts everything.

So every one of these separates what happened from what somebody concluded, and keeps them separate all the way to the output.

## Why these five

**Pipeline review.** Every pipeline is too big and every forecast is too optimistic, and everyone in the room knows both. The Monday meeting then argues about whichever deal is loudest.

**Deal qualification.** A deal at 80 percent usually means the rep feels good about it. The questions that actually decide it are boring, specific, and skipped because the answers might be bad.

**Discovery and debrief.** Calls get prepped in four minutes and never debriefed, so what was not learned stays invisible until it surfaces as a security review nobody knew about.

**Account plan.** Written annually for a review, in a template, never reopened. Meanwhile the revenue sits in one product, one unit and one relationship.

**Forecast commit.** A number submitted with no working behind it gets defended monthly in a meeting where the only moves are to hold it or lose credibility.

## They work together

Each stands alone. In sequence they cover a selling cycle:

```
Before the call     →  Discovery Plan          →  questions in the right order
After the call      →  Call Debrief            →  what is still unknown, as an agenda
One deal            →  Deal Qualification      →  the stage the evidence supports
The whole pipeline  →  Pipeline Review         →  which four deals decide the quarter
The deal list       →  Forecast Commit         →  a number you can put your name on
An existing account →  Account Plan            →  where it grows, and where it is exposed
```

Three pairings worth knowing. The debrief feeds the deal review, because a call is only useful once it has become qualification data. The deal review feeds the forecast, because a commit tier needs criteria applied consistently rather than deal by deal. And the pipeline review tells you which deals are worth a full qualification review at all, since running one on ninety deals is not the job. All of this sits downstream of the positioning work, which decides what you can claim and prove; none of these skills invents a product claim.

## What makes these different from asking directly

**Evidence and inference never share a column.** "They sent the security questionnaire" and "they seem keen" are different kinds of thing, and once inference is written down as fact nobody downstream remembers which it was.

**"Assumed" does not get softened to "claimed".** Something the customer said is a claim worth testing. The same words inferred from their enthusiasm is a guess, and most deal reviews collapse the two because it sounds better out loud. A champion is tested the same way, on what they have actually done rather than how they seem, and doing nothing and building it internally are counted as competitors, because both win more deals than any named rival.

**Coverage gets computed twice.** Once on the whole pipeline, and again after removing the parked deals, the repeatedly slipped ones and the ones with no next step. The gap between those two numbers is the finding, and no single coverage figure will ever show it.

**A stage probability is never presented as a forecast, and the close date comes from their paperwork.** Weighting a pipeline is arithmetic performed on guesses. Legal, security, procurement, onboarding and purchase order each have a duration, and their sum is the earliest date physically available, which moves more close dates than anything else in a deal review.

**Unknown is not the same as no opportunity.** In a whitespace grid, a cell nobody has asked about needs research; a cell where the need does not exist needs nothing. Presenting both as pipeline is how account plans produce nothing.

**Every rate carries its sample size, and silence is treated as information.** A conversion rate on nine deals is not a rate. No activity in sixty days, a champion gone quiet, a next step with no date: these are the strongest predictors in any export, and usually the emptiest fields in it.

## Getting started

1. Open Settings in Claude, go to **Capabilities**, and turn on **code execution and file creation**. The pipeline review and the account plan need it. The other three do not, so most of this set runs on a free plan.
2. Take one skill's folder and compress it to a `.zip`. The file inside must stay named `SKILL.md`, because that is what Claude looks for. The guide beside it is not needed and can be left out.
3. Go to **Customize**, then **Skills**, then add a skill and upload the zip.
4. Repeat for each skill you want.

Skills you upload stay private to your account. Once installed they work in Chat and Cowork, and Claude picks the right one from what you ask.

**Where to start.** Run the deal qualification review on a deal you are confident about. Seeing your evidence column next to your inference column is the fastest way to understand what this set does, and it takes five minutes.

## Using them

Describe the deal, or upload the export, the way you would to a manager.

- "Q3 pipeline, target is 4.2 crore. Is this real?"
- "Deal at 80 percent closing this month. Here is what has happened. What am I missing?"
- "Discovery call tomorrow with a head of operations. Plan it."
- "Our biggest account, QBR next month. Where is the risk and where is the growth?"
- "Target is 1.8 crore, I can defend 1.1. Write the commit brief."

Saying what actually happened, rather than how you feel about it, is what changes the output.

## A note on personal data

CRM exports, deal notes and call transcripts carry names, email addresses, phone numbers and job titles.

Every skill says so once if it finds them, notes that the analysis needs roles, accounts, stages, values and dates rather than identities, and offers to work with contact columns removed or names replaced by roles. Then it continues with whatever you decide, without repeating itself.

## What these will not do

- **No writing the outreach.** No cold email, follow-up, message or objection script. Those already work unaided.
- **No accepting your number.** Where the evidence does not support a stage, a close date or a commit, these say so and give the ones it does support.
- **No inflating or deflating a forecast.** Sandbagging and optimism are the same failure, and both get named.
- **No pricing, discount or contract advice**, which goes to whoever owns commercial approval, and **no product claims**: nothing here invents a capability, a proof point or a customer result.
- **No words in the customer's mouth.** What is not in your notes is not in the output, and what you concluded is labelled as your conclusion.
- **No judging a rep.** Deals get assessed, not people, and coaching notes attach to moments in one call.
- **No system access, and nothing is sent, logged or updated.** These work on exports and notes you provide, never connecting to your CRM, dialler or email, and every output is for a person to review and act on.

- A Claude account, with code execution turned on for two of the five
