# Educators Skills for Claude

Five skills for the work that happens after teaching: the paper, the marking, the analysis, the plan and the report. The evening work, not the classroom.

They run inside your own Claude account, on mark sheets you export and work you paste. Nothing to install on your machine, nothing to connect, no coding.

---

## Who these are for

Anyone who teaches and then answers for it: school teachers across every subject and board, coaching and test-prep faculty, college and university lecturers, heads of department and academic coordinators, exam cells, private tutors, corporate trainers and L&D professionals, instructional designers, and counsellors preparing for parent meetings.

Four of the five need no export from any system. They work on what you paste or dictate, so a teacher with no access to the school software can use them from the first day.

## The five skills

| Skill | Give it | Get back |
|---|---|---|
| **[Assessment and Item Analysis](edu-assessment-analysis/edu-assessment-analysis-guide.md)** | A question-wise mark sheet | Item statistics, topic and cognitive-level gaps, students grouped by what they missed, and a reteach plan triaged against your actual time |
| **[Marking and Feedback Pack](edu-marking-feedback/edu-marking-feedback-guide.md)** | Student work plus your rubric | Marks against your standard, what the next level needed, and the cohort view of what the class got wrong |
| **[Question Paper and Blueprint](edu-question-paper/edu-question-paper-guide.md)** | Topics, marks, duration | A blueprint confirmed first, a paper written to it, a marking scheme, and a time check. Or a review of an existing paper |
| **[Lesson and Unit Plan](edu-lesson-plan/edu-lesson-plan-guide.md)** | Topic, time, level | A plan built backwards from the check, misconceptions named, and what to cut decided in advance |
| **[Progress Report and Parent Communication](edu-progress-report/edu-progress-report-guide.md)** | The evidence you already have | Comments anchored to evidence, about the work, each with an action and an owner |

Each skill has its own guide with the exact inputs it needs, the phrasings that trigger it, what good output looks like, its limits, and a troubleshooting table.

## The line none of these cross

**Nothing labels the learner.**

These skills assess work. They do not assess the person who did it. No comment on ability, effort, attitude or potential appears anywhere, and nothing here predicts what a student will become.

Refusing to diagnose is the easy half. The harder half is the same rule applied under time pressure at ten at night. "Careless" is faster to write than a description of the work. "Fourteen weak students" is faster than naming the gap those fourteen share. "Unlikely to cope with higher maths next year" feels like useful advice to a parent. All three are the same failure, and a child carries them for years.

So every skill here is built the same way. It describes what is on the page and what to do next, and where the work suggests something a professional should look at, it describes the pattern and names who is qualified to look. It does not conclude.

## Student data stays out by default

These inputs are about children, so this set works differently from the rest of the library.

**De-identified is the default, not the option.** Each skill names the identifying fields before doing anything, and none needs a name to work: roll numbers or initials carry through every one, and report comments can be drafted against roll numbers with the names reinstated by you. No skill asks for an identifier it does not need, and none tells you whether you may share a file, because that is your institution's data policy rather than something a skill can rule on. Stated once, in place, then the work continues with whatever you decide.

**Nothing here is addressed to a student or a parent until you decide it is.** Every output comes back to the teacher as a draft.

## Why these five

**Item analysis.** The marks get entered, the average gets announced, and the class moves on. Which questions were broken and which ideas the class missed is in that mark sheet and takes an evening to extract.

**Marking.** Sixty scripts is a weekend, and the comments are the first thing sacrificed. So students get a mark and repeat the mistake. And nobody tallies what the whole class got wrong, which is the most valuable output of the entire exercise.

**Question paper.** Written the week it is needed, from memory, with the blueprint reverse-engineered afterwards to match. Then two ambiguous questions are found simultaneously by two hundred candidates.

**Lesson plan.** A list of activities with times against them, missing the one thing it exists for: how you will know before the bell whether the class learned it. **Report comments.** Forty in one evening, so they come out generic, when the comment that would change something at home is exactly the same length.

## They work together

Each stands alone. In sequence they cover a teaching cycle:

```
Topic and time      →  Lesson and Unit Plan     →  a plan with a check you could fail
Syllabus and marks  →  Question Paper           →  a paper that fits its blueprint and its time
The scripts         →  Marking and Feedback      →  what the class got wrong, not just the marks
The mark sheet      →  Assessment Analysis       →  what to reteach, and what to drop
The evidence        →  Progress Report           →  a comment a parent can act on
```

Three pairings worth knowing. Run the item analysis before planning a reteach lesson, so the plan uses your class's actual misconception rather than a general one; the marking pack's recurring misconceptions feed the same place. And the paper's blueprint is what the item analysis checks compliance against, so building one makes the next analysis sharper.

## What makes these different from asking directly

**It separates a bad question from a bad result, and groups by gap rather than rank.** Low score plus negative discrimination means strong students got it wrong at the same rate as weak ones, which is a paper problem and not a class problem; reteaching on that evidence is wasted time, and almost nobody computes the number that reveals it. And a mark sheet already gives you a rank order; what it cannot give you is which fourteen students can be taught together because they missed the same thing.

**Every criterion says what the next level needed.** A student who knows they got 3 out of 5 has learned nothing. One who knows what the fourth mark required can go and get it.

**Wrong, missing and unclear are kept apart, and you get two next steps rather than fifteen.** A misconception returned as "be more careful" survives intact to the next test, while an omission is exam technique and needs a different conversation. And a script covered in comments is a script whose feedback will not be read.

**The blueprint comes before the questions, and the time gets tested.** Writing first and reverse-engineering a blueprint is what produces a paper that is 80 percent recall without anybody choosing it. Papers are routinely set that cannot be finished, and lesson plans written for a period longer than the one that exists. Both get checked, and what does not fit gets named.

**Every check carries a branch, and every comment has to fail the swap test.** A check for understanding with no stated response if the class fails it is decoration. And if you change the name on a report comment and it still reads true, it was never a comment about that student.

**Nothing is scored that should not be.** It will not give you a plagiarism or AI-use score. Those tools are unreliable and a false accusation against a child is not a recoverable harm. You get what is observable, described, and referred to you.

## Getting started

1. Open Settings in Claude, go to **Capabilities**, and turn on **code execution and file creation**. Only the item analysis needs it. The other four work without it, so most of this set runs on a free plan.
2. Take one skill's folder and compress it to a `.zip`. The file inside must stay named `SKILL.md`, because that is what Claude looks for. The guide beside it is not needed and can be left out.
3. Go to **Customize**, then **Skills**, then add a skill and upload the zip.
4. Repeat for each skill you want.

Skills you upload stay private to your account. Once installed they work in Chat and Cowork, and Claude picks the right one from what you ask.

**Where to start.** Run the item analysis on a test you have already marked and moved on from. It usually finds a question that measured nothing, which is the fastest way to see what this set is for.

## Using them

Upload the mark sheet or paste the work, and describe the task the way you would to a colleague.

- "Unit test marks, question-wise, 38 students. What do I reteach?"
- "Six answers and the marking scheme. Mark them and tell me what the class got wrong."
- "Unit test, 40 marks, 90 minutes, chapters 3 to 5. Our blueprint is attached."
- "38 report comments for parents, 200 characters each. Marks and my notes attached."

## What these will not do

- **No judgement about the learner.** No comment on ability, effort, attitude or potential, and no prediction about what a student will become.
- **No plagiarism or AI-use score.** Unreliable tools, and an unrecoverable harm if wrong. What is observable is described and referred to you.
- **No diagnosis.** These do not identify, suggest or rule out a learning difficulty, and do not recommend an assessment. Where a pattern is unusual they describe it and name who is qualified to look.
- **No comparison, rank or prediction** in a report. Students are compared against the standard and against their own earlier work, and nothing else.
- **No marks awarded, and no grouping by ability.** Marking is a recommendation against your rubric for you to confirm, and support and extension are described by the need they meet rather than by what a child is assumed capable of.
- **No system access, and nothing sent.** These work on exports and text you provide, never connecting to your school software, and every output is a draft for you to check and own.

## What you need

- A Claude account, with code execution turned on for the item analysis
- Mark sheets in Excel or CSV, and student work pasted or typed
- Nothing else. No installation, no terminal, no coding, nothing to connect
