# Repurposing and Channel Adaptation

Repurposing usually means the same paragraph pasted into five places with the character count adjusted. Every channel then underperforms, and the conclusion drawn is that the content was weak. What was actually weak was the translation into each channel's native form, and nobody looked at that because it does not look like a decision anyone made.

## When to use this

Trigger on: repurpose, repurposing, atomise, content atomisation, multi-channel, distribution plan, "turn this into", "make this into posts", "we need this for LinkedIn and Instagram", newsletter from a blog, clips from a webinar.

## Before you start

Required: the source asset. A blog post, report, webinar transcript, podcast episode, deck, or long interview.

Ask, briefly:
1. Which channels, and **which of them actually matter**. A list of eight channels usually means none of them is being done properly.
2. Who runs each channel and what capacity exists. A plan requiring video nobody can edit is not a plan.
3. Whether the source is already published, and where. A derivative that competes with its own source in search is a self-inflicted problem.
4. What the source was for, and whether the derivatives share that goal or serve a different one.

## Workflow

### 1. Find the atomic ideas before touching any channel

Read the source and extract every idea that stands alone, meaning something a reader could take, understand and use without the rest of the piece.

Most long assets contain three to six of these, not thirty. **Being honest about the count is the entire discipline here.** An asset with four ideas generates four strong derivatives and forty weak ones, and the forty weak ones cost more attention than they return.

For each idea, note what makes it worth attention on its own: a number, a counterintuitive claim, a concrete example, a mistake, or a comparison.

### 2. Match idea to channel, rather than pushing everything everywhere

Each channel rewards a different shape, so state the reason for every placement:

- **Long text**, such as a newsletter or an article: an idea that needs an argument and can carry a worked example
- **Short text**, such as a single post: one idea, one proof, one line of consequence. Anything needing two proofs does not fit.
- **Carousel or slides:** a sequence, a process, or a comparison. Not a paragraph split across ten slides.
- **Short video:** one idea with something to show. An idea that has to be explained rather than demonstrated does not belong here.
- **Long video or podcast:** the argument with the digressions kept, which is exactly what the text version had to cut
- **Visual or chart:** a relationship between numbers. Not a list with a background colour.

An idea that does not fit a channel's shape has not been adapted for that channel. Say so, and leave the slot empty rather than filling it.

### 3. Rewrite for the channel, never truncate

This is the failure the whole skill exists to prevent. Truncation keeps the opening and loses the point, because in long form the point arrives after the setup, and in short form it has to arrive first.

For each derivative:

- **Restructure the hook.** Long form earns attention with its first paragraph. Short form has to earn it in the first line, and that line is very often a sentence from the middle of the source.
- **Rebuild the ending.** A blog post ends with a synthesis. A post ends with something a reader can respond to.
- **Change the evidence density.** Long form supports a claim with three examples. Short form uses the best one and cuts the rest.
- **Adjust the register** to the channel, staying inside the brand's range rather than stepping outside it.

Never carry a reference to the source's own structure into a derivative. "As we discussed above" reveals a paste, and readers notice.

### 4. Decide what must not be repurposed

Say this explicitly, because nobody else will:

- Anything whose value is its completeness. A methodology, a full comparison, a detailed procedure. Fragmenting it produces something that misleads.
- Anything time-bound whose window has closed
- Anything needing context the channel cannot carry, particularly anything sensitive, regulated, or easily misread out of context
- Anything that channel covered recently

### 5. Sequence the distribution

- What goes first, and why. Usually the strongest idea on the channel with the most reach.
- Spacing, so derivatives do not compete with each other for the same audience on the same day
- **Which derivative points back to the source and which stands alone.** Not everything should link. A post that exists only to drive a click reads like one.
- Where a derivative should wait for a signal from an earlier one

### 6. Keep the source canonical

Where derivatives could compete with the source in search, name which asset is the source of record and how the others should reference it. Republishing a post wholesale on another platform is the common version of this mistake.

### 7. Say what to watch, without promising a number

For each channel, state the metric that would show the derivative worked, and what result would mean this idea does not travel there. Never predict a number, because an invented figure gets quoted back later as though it were a forecast.

## Output format

**1. Atomic ideas.** The honest count, each with what makes it stand alone.

**2. Idea to channel map.**

| Idea | Channel | Form | Why this channel | Who produces it |

**3. The derivatives.** Each written for its channel, not truncated.

**4. Do not repurpose.** What stays whole, and why.

**5. Sequence.** Order, spacing, and which ones link back.

**6. Canonical note.** Which asset is the source of record.

**7. What to watch.** The metric per channel, and what would mean the idea does not travel.

**8. Capacity check.** What this plan requires against what the team said it has.

## Quality bar

- Is the atomic idea count honest, rather than inflated to fill the channel list
- Is every derivative rewritten for its channel rather than truncated
- Does every short derivative carry its point in the first line
- Does every placement have a stated reason beyond the channel existing
- Is there a "do not repurpose" section with something actually in it
- Does any derivative still refer to the source's own structure
- Can this team produce this with the people and the time they have
- Is the canonical asset named wherever derivatives could compete with it
- Is there no promised number anywhere in the output

## What this skill does not do

- It does not schedule or publish anything.
- It does not add new claims or evidence to a derivative. Everything in one traces back to the source, and where a derivative needs support the source lacks, that becomes a query.
- It does not produce video, audio or design. It specifies what each has to contain and do.
- It does not promise reach or engagement for any derivative.
- It will not fill a channel it has no material for. An empty slot is the honest answer, and a weak derivative costs more than no derivative.
