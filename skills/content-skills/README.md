# Content Skills for Claude

Five skills for the system around content rather than the writing of it: the standard, the brief, the check before publishing, the distribution, and the decision about what already exists.

They run inside your own Claude account, on drafts you paste and exports you already pull. Nothing to install on your machine, nothing to connect, no coding.

---

## Who these are for

People who produce content on a deadline and answer for it: content writers and copywriters, content leads and managers, editors, SEO and organic growth managers, social media managers, brand and communications managers, newsletter writers, video and podcast producers, agency content teams, freelance writers, and solo creators.

If your week involves a brief that was one sentence, a draft due tomorrow, and a library nobody has looked at in a year, these are built for it.

## The five skills

| Skill | Give it | Get back |
|---|---|---|
| **[Voice Guide and Consistency Check](content-voice-guide/content-voice-guide-guide.md)** | Samples of writing that is the voice, and some that is not | A guide of testable do-and-not rules, a lexicon, a register range, and a check mode for drafts |
| **[Content Brief Builder](content-brief/content-brief-guide.md)** | A vague request | One question, the reader, the intent, the angle, the structure, and the evidence required before writing |
| **[Pre-publication Check](content-prepublication-check/content-prepublication-check-guide.md)** | The draft | Every claim registered with its source status, numbers verified, headline tested against body, flags routed |
| **[Repurposing and Channel Adaptation](content-repurposing/content-repurposing-guide.md)** | One long asset, and the channels you actually run | The ideas that stand alone, matched to channels and rewritten natively rather than truncated |
| **[Content Performance Audit](content-performance-audit/content-performance-audit-guide.md)** | A performance export | One decision per asset, cannibalising clusters found first, ranked by return |

Each skill has its own guide with the exact inputs it needs, the phrasings that trigger it, what good output looks like, its limits, and a troubleshooting table.

## Why you will not find "write me a blog post" here

Because asking any AI for a blog post already works. A skill there would add ceremony, not capability.

What does not already work is everything around the writing. Knowing which question the piece should answer before somebody starts drafting. Having a voice standard specific enough that two writers produce similar work. Catching the plausible statistic that has no source behind it. Turning one asset into four strong derivatives instead of thirty weak ones. And deciding what to do about the two hundred pieces already published.

That is the content system, and it is where content work is actually won or lost. The writing is the easy half.

## The line none of these cross

**No unearned authority.**

Nothing here claims experience the brand did not have, cites a source that does not exist, or passes off work that is not theirs.

The obvious version is the fabricated statistic, which is now the characteristic failure of AI-assisted content: fluent, plausible, specific and completely wrong, which is exactly the combination that gets published. So every claim in a draft gets registered with a source status, and "we tested 40 tools" gets interrogated on whether anybody tested 40 tools.

The quieter version is the brief that asks for authority and supplies no evidence, which pushes a writer on deadline toward inventing one. That is a briefing failure, not a writer failure, so the brief specifies what has to be cited and where each item comes from before anybody writes.

## Why these five

**Voice guide.** Almost every guide says friendly, professional and approachable, which describes no voice and rules nothing out, so it cannot be used to reject anything. Then each writer guesses and every review becomes an argument about taste.

**Content brief.** "We need a blog post about X" is what most writers receive. The revision cycle that follows argues about things that could have been settled in twenty minutes before anybody wrote a word.

**Pre-publication check.** The person about to publish is the person who wrote it, so the checks only an outsider would run are the ones that get skipped.

**Repurposing.** Truncation keeps the opening and loses the point, because in long form the point arrives after the setup. Then the channel gets blamed for the content.

**Performance audit.** Two hundred pieces exist, ten carry the traffic, and the default response is to publish a two hundred and first, which is the one action guaranteed not to help.

## They work together

Each stands alone. In sequence they cover a content cycle:

```
Samples             →  Voice Guide            →  a standard two writers can both hit
A vague request     →  Content Brief          →  a brief a freelancer can execute
The draft           →  Pre-publication Check  →  a verdict, not a list of suggestions
The published asset →  Repurposing            →  four strong derivatives, not thirty weak ones
The whole library   →  Performance Audit      →  one decision per asset, ranked by return
```

Three pairings worth knowing. Build the voice guide first, because the brief references it and the pre-publication check applies it. Run the performance audit before planning a quarter, since it usually says consolidate rather than produce. And the audit's cannibalisation groups tell you which briefs not to write at all. These also sit downstream of the positioning and customer language work: that decides what you can claim and in whose words, and these decide how it gets said and whether the piece stands up.

## What makes these different from asking directly

**Rules come as pairs, or they are not rules.** "Be concise" cannot be applied to a draft. "Cut the first sentence, it is almost always throat-clearing" can. Every rule in a voice guide gets a do and a not, both concrete, both drawn from your own samples.

**No adjectives in a voice guide.** The output must not contain friendly, professional, approachable or authentic anywhere, because those are what people write instead of doing the work.

**Claims get registered before the piece gets read.** Every factual assertion is extracted mechanically, separate from reading for quality, which is what surfaces the claims that read as background and are in fact assertions nobody checked.

**Percentages get their base checked.** A rise of 30 percent from 1 to 1.3 percent is a very different claim from 30 to 39 percent, and only one of them is worth a headline.

**Intent decides structure.** A piece answering a "how do I" question with an explanation fails however well it is written, and no editing rescues it. That gets decided in the brief, not in review.

**The angle test is one question.** Could a competitor publish this identical piece? If yes, it is not an angle, and the brief says so rather than letting a generic piece be commissioned and then rejected for being generic.

**Derivatives are rewritten, never truncated, and the counts are honest.** Short form has to carry its point in the first line, and that line is usually a sentence from the middle of the source. Most long assets hold three to six ideas that genuinely stand alone, not thirty, and a channel with no material for it comes back empty, because a weak derivative costs more attention than no derivative.

**Nothing promises a lift.** No refresh gets a predicted recovery percentage and no derivative gets predicted reach. You get the metric to watch instead, because an invented number gets quoted back later as a forecast.

**They tell you the honest limit.** The pre-publication check is not a plagiarism scanner and says so, then tells you when to run a real one. The audit says when it cannot separate decay from never-worked, and what that limits.

## Getting started

1. Open Settings in Claude, go to **Capabilities**, and turn on **code execution and file creation**. Only the performance audit needs it; the other four work without it, so most of this set runs on a free plan.
2. Take one skill's folder and compress it to a `.zip`. The file inside must stay named `SKILL.md`, because that is what Claude looks for. The guide beside it is not needed and can be left out.
3. Go to **Customize**, then **Skills**, then add a skill and upload the zip.
4. Repeat for each skill you want.

Skills you upload stay private to your account. Once installed they work in Chat and Cowork, and Claude picks the right one from what you ask.

**Where to start.** Run the pre-publication check on a piece you already published and were happy with. The claim register is almost always longer than expected, and it is the fastest way to see what this set catches.

## Using them

Paste the draft or upload the export, and describe the task the way you would to a colleague.

- "Eight of our posts. Three are on-voice, five are not. Build the guide."
- "My boss wants a post about AI in supply chain. That is all I have been told."
- "Check this before it goes out. Some of it was drafted with AI."
- "Webinar transcript, 50 minutes. We run LinkedIn and a newsletter, nothing else."
- "206 blog posts and six months of Search Console. What do we keep and what do we kill?"

## What these will not do

- **No writing the piece.** These produce the standard, the brief, the check and the distribution. Drafting is what Claude already does well unaided.
- **No invented evidence.** No fabricated statistic, source, quote or claimed experience. An unsupported claim makes a draft "not ready", however good the writing is.
- **No plagiarism scanning.** The pre-publication check flags the signals and tells you when to run an actual tool. It is explicit about what it did not check.
- **No legal, medical, financial or regulatory rulings.** Those get flagged and routed to whoever owns them in your organisation.
- **No keyword research or search volume**, and **no promised lift or reach** for any change, refresh or derivative. Intent and structure come from the question and from whatever you paste in.
- **No system access**, and **nothing is scheduled, published or sent.** They work on drafts you paste and exports you provide, never connecting to your CMS, analytics or any platform, and every output is for a person to review and act on.

## What you need

- A Claude account, with code execution turned on for the performance audit
- Your drafts and samples pasted in, and your exports in Excel or CSV
- Nothing else. No installation, no terminal, no coding, nothing to connect
